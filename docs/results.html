What it isthe pixelwise union of two independent detector families: the 11-fold U-Net++/DeepLabV3+ ensemble (172,974 px, widest coverage) and the 6-fold blend of a different model set (21,492 px, 3.45× the random floor per pixel). Built by scripts/union_submission.py. The union is the best of the three on the third-party protocol (L 0.1222 vs 0.1189/0.1188), on the held-out segments with the catalogue charged (S 0.1128) and under the organizers' mask (P 0.0903 vs 0.0887/0.0831), and it stays the best of the three once the hidden new-fault set is at least 0.99× the size of the local stand-in (the ratio is computed from the measured weighted terms, not typed). It is not the best on the strictest local truth population (I), where the 6-fold arm alone wins 0.1173 to 0.0955 — which is why that arm is offered below as an alternate rather than hidden. No other candidate this repository holds, including the one the site used to lead with, beats either arm under the organizers' mask.

The single most important row is the one that collapses. The binary field the TURBO_README cites as the best local model scores L 0.4666 and P 0.0081 — because 33,623 of its 62,908 emitted pixels sit on the supplied catalogue. Under the third-party protocol those pixels are forgiven (they are inside the training buffer) and under the organizers' mask they are worth nothing; a uniform random emission of the same size scores 0.0393 where it scores 0.0081. scripts/generate_turbo_variants.py built it with a proximity boost exp(-d/decay) plus a forced 0.95 on known-fault pixels: 50.9 % of its pixels lie within 3 px of the supplied catalogue. It is a label echo, and every table on this site that ranked it first was ranking the echo. The site's previous primary candidate (gemsdoe2_recall_union_submission.tif) sits at P 0.0626 with a 0.97× lift — i.e. at the random floor.

Controls on the same population, so the columns can be read: perfect_field 1.0000 · truth_dilated_1px 0.8814 · supplied_catalogue_echo 1.0000 · a uniform random emission of the same size is the floor, not zero, because the metric's TP term is a max over the 300 m cone. Source: data/evidence/submission_portfolio.json, written by scripts/measure_submission_portfolio.py.

Two things that did not work, measured

Pruning the emission does not help. The metric's loss is dominated by emission that earns nothing (on the canonical 11-fold field: TP 4,146 against 0.2×FP 32,778), so the obvious move is to keep only the pixels another signal corroborates — the second model family, long components, or the label-free structure-tensor lineament field. Every one of those policies was scored on a tune half and the winner measured once on the other half (scripts/prune_submission_emission.py, grid pre-registered in the script):

The winner on the tune half was the 6-fold blend alone; on the untouched measure half it scored P 0.0493 against 0.0469 for the unpruned artifact — a tie, i.e. no policy in the grid beat doing nothing. The lineament-corroborated policies were actively worse (P 0.0149–0.0226). Emission selection is not where the remaining score is; this repository's field is already a one-pixel skeleton (skeletonize returns the shipped mask unchanged), so there is no thickness left to remove.

The large gain from a near-catalogue band is an artefact of the proxy, and is deliberately not shipped. Restricting the emission to within a few pixels of the supplied catalogue roughly doubles the local P score, monotonically, because the proxy's "held-out" truth is carved out of the supplied catalogue and therefore lies on the pixels the platform masks — so any field that hugs the catalogue's edge looks good for a reason that says nothing about the hidden new-fault set.

Honest status

A green workflow is not evidence. The 6-fold ensemble run 35042805806 trained every fold for 2 h 36 min and then crashed while writing the submission (RasterBlockError: the height and width of TIFF dataset blocks must be multiples of 16). The job was reported successful, because the step piped Python into tee without pipefail, and the evidence it committed was self-incriminating: data/evidence/runs/35042805806/submission.tif is 110 bytes (a GDAL stub) and validation.log is an argparse usage dump. Fixed on 2026-09-16: a read-back-verifying writer (src/submission_io.py), pipefail on every piped step, and a commit gate that ships FAILED.json instead of a stub. The rebuilt submission is the newest run below.
The pipeline runs end to end on the real competition rasters and every number below is measured, but it is not yet competitive: the first full-scale run was a 2-epoch MobileNetV2 smoke test that scored below the trivial blanket-coverage baseline, which is recorded here as a negative result rather than dressed up. And every DTI on this page is computed against the provided labels or local SGMC proxy populations; none is the hidden scored universe (see Metric).

Cross-catalogue transfer — measured, and REFUSED

Catalogue A (SGMC structure, DOI 10.3133/ds1052) was to be emitted and scored against catalogue B (QFaults layer 21, DOI 10.5066/P9BCVRCK) on B’s code-2 pixels — the traces the training labels do not contain. The runner fetched B and rasterised it on the competition grid, and that population turned out to be one pixel wide.

Verdict: REFUSED - catalogue B is NOT independent of the training labels: 60,938 of its 60,939 in-footprint pixels (100.00 %) are already within R = 3 px of a label, leaving 1 px (0.0016 %) as a 'new fault' population - below the pre-registered minimum of 100 px and 0.50 %. Scoring against 1 pixel(s) would produce a number with no meaning, so none is reported. This is a FINDING about the two catalogues, not a bug: it means the training labels and catalogue B are the same lines in this footprint, and it is recorded with the overlap that establishes it.

Why this exits 0 and not 1. “These two catalogues are the same lines” is a finding about the data, so it is committed as a report with the overlap that establishes it (data/evidence/xcat/transfer_report.json, input sha256s included). A genuinely broken B raster — under --min-b-all-px 1,000 in-footprint pixels, i.e. empty, misaligned or miscoded — still fails the run and writes nothing, so a defect can never masquerade as a result.

What this closes. Rules §3.3 says the scored faults are a new expert set, and §11 item 1 of the executive summary asked for a prior on recall against it. That prior cannot come from a second Quaternary catalogue: none is independent of these labels, and that is now measured rather than argued. The SGMC proxy population (data/evidence/proxy/proxy_catalogue.tif, 61,664 code-2 px, 24.94% already covered by the labels) remains the only available surrogate for “faults the labels lack”, with its structural weakness intact — pre-Quaternary bedrock structure digitised from state geologic maps, not an expert interpretation of the GeoDAWN geophysics. Any future candidate catalogue must be checked for disjointness with scripts/build_proxy_catalogue.py before a transfer number is quoted.

What the numbers mean

  • DTI shaped > DTI raw on every run so far — the floor-plus-thinning step consistently helps, exactly as the metric algebra predicts.
  • FP_w dominates. The smoke model emits far too much diffuse mass. This is the single biggest lever available, and it is an output shaping problem as much as a modelling one.
  • Local scores are optimistic. They are computed against the labels used in training and are not the private test set.

Next steps, in priority order

  1. Optimise for the scored universe, not the catalogue. The scored faults are missing from labels.tif, so model selection, shaping calibration and fold weighting must be anchored on src/discovery.py diagnostics plus a spatial-block hold-out instead of catalogue DTI. This is a measurement-protocol change before it is a modelling change.
  2. Parallel 6-fold ensemble on CPU runners — implemented as the Train MC ensemble workflow (configs/config_ci_ensemble.yaml: UNet++ / DeepLabV3+ / U-Net × resnet34 at patch 256, 8-way TTA, pooled shaping calibration). The first full run is the current frontier until a GPU exists.
